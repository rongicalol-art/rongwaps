Kind: strip
Style: objects
Characters: none

SUBJECT: a 3-panel picture strip for a listening question ("which picture matches what you hear?").
LAYOUT: landscape 3:2 canvas (1536x1024). Three EQUAL square panels side by side in one horizontal row, vertically centered, separated by wide plain WHITE gutters (at least 40px) and a plain white margin around all of them. Each panel is a self-contained square scene. Do not number or label the panels.
ALL THREE PANELS share the same base drawing: the same simple room wall seen straight on, soft mint paint, with ONE big window with white frames in the upper middle of the wall, blue sky outside, and the same light wooden floor. Directly UNDER the window stands ONE piece of furniture, and only that piece changes:
- Panel 1 (left): a single BED under the window (white pillow, blue blanket).
- Panel 2 (middle): a coral two-seat SOFA under the window.
- Panel 3 (right): a TV on a low wooden cabinet under the window. The TV stands on a visible stand or base, its screen shows soft abstract colors (no words, no picture of people), and it is clearly smaller than the window above it, so it cannot be mistaken for a second window or a black box.
The furniture is large and centered so the difference is obvious at a glance. Each panel has only that one piece of furniture and NO OTHER FURNITURE AT ALL: no bed, sofa, armchair, chair, table, shelf, lamp or rug anywhere else, and in the TV panel nothing faces the TV (no sofa or chair in front of it). The rest of each room is empty floor and wall. No people, no animals, no text.

FROM REVIEW (overrides the base drawing above where they differ): Draw the same room as a real lit interior in a slight three-quarter view: a wall corner, a baseboard, the one furniture piece with its cast shadow, still no other furniture, the same room in all three panels. Light it like the benchmark: a window with a city-and-trees view, hard-edged flat shapes of sunlight across the wall and floor or table, a cast shadow under the object, depth from a wall corner or baseboard, cobalt, coral and yellow accents. Never a lone object on a flat wall-and-table-edge strip.
