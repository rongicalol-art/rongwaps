SUBJECT: a 3-panel picture strip for a listening question ("which picture matches what you hear?").
LAYOUT: landscape 3:2 canvas (1536x1024). Three EQUAL square panels side by side in one horizontal row, vertically centered, separated by wide plain WHITE gutters (at least 40px) and a plain white margin around all of them. Each panel is a self-contained square scene. Do not number or label the panels.
ALL THREE PANELS share the same base drawing: the same simple teal two-seat sofa seen from the front in a slightly high three-quarter view looking down, standing on the same light wooden floor, the same soft cream wall behind, the same small yellow cushion at the left end of the seat. The sofa has TALL, open legs (about a quarter of the sofa's height), so the floor under it is clearly visible. Nothing else in the room. Only the place of ONE closed BROWN wallet changes (brown, not red), and the wallet is drawn large enough to see at a glance:
- Panel 1 (left): the brown wallet lies ON the sofa seat, in the middle of the seat.
- Panel 2 (middle): the brown wallet lies on the floor UNDER the sofa, centred between the sofa legs, with the floor visible all around it.
- Panel 3 (right): the brown wallet lies on the floor NEXT TO the sofa, beside its right end, at least one full wallet-width away from the sofa, not under it.
Same sofa, same cushion, same floor and wall in all three panels; exactly one wallet in every panel. No people, no text.

FROM REVIEW (overrides the base drawing above where they differ): Draw the identical sofa at the same size, angle and position in all three panels, left-aligned, with empty floor to its right in every panel. Panel 2: the wallet fully behind the line of the front feet, centred between the legs, entirely in the sofa's shadow. Panel 3: more than one wallet-width of bare floor between the sofa's right arm or leg and the wallet. Only the sofa, its cushion and the wallet in the room. Light it like the benchmark: a window with a city-and-trees view, hard-edged flat shapes of sunlight across the wall and floor or table, a cast shadow under the object, depth from a wall corner or baseboard, cobalt, coral and yellow accents. Never a lone object on a flat wall-and-table-edge strip.
