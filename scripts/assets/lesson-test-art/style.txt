The attached approved classroom illustration is the visual benchmark for this whole series (the app's lesson covers): match its rendering, saturation, color, shape language and character proportions exactly, not its content or facial expressions. Adult university students, not children. Keep the same illustration system across the whole series.

Style: Kurzgesagt-inspired approachable flat vector illustration, rounded geometric forms, smooth curves, simplified expressive faces, crisp shape boundaries and confident single-hue color blocks. Keep eyes open with a clear natural gaze by default; use closed eyes only for an explicitly requested blink, sleep or wink. Expressions should feel natural and suited to the scene; avoid repeated broad smiles and keep mouths closed by default. Draw mouths as simple single-line marks, with no detailed lips or facial anatomy. Extremely saturated colors with strong warm/cool contrast: deep teal, indigo and cobalt against coral, golden yellow and cyan; occasional magenta accents. Let each picture choose a dominant pair rather than using every hue equally. Favor solid flat color, with sparse hard-edged shadow shapes and only the subtle tonal variation visible in the approved example. No conspicuous gradients, grain, texture, painterly shading, heavy outlines, anime styling, photorealism or 3D.

Art direction: clearly composed. Establish foreground, subject and background through overlap and scale, like the benchmark. Give props physical places in the scene; never draw an isolated icon or clip-art object on an empty background, and do not scatter floating icons around people: even when the brief is about one object, it sits in a real place. Backgrounds support the subject with a few deliberate shapes. Keep faces, hands and the key object comfortably within frame and readable at thumbnail size.

No dialogue balloons, captions, titles, labels, readable writing, fake Chinese, logos, watermarks or UI overlays, unless this brief explicitly gives the exact text. Where a picture needs a clock, show an unnumbered analog face with the specified hands. Simple background extras only when the brief needs them; keep attention on the subject.
