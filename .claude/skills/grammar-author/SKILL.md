# Grammar author

> **Exercise pass is live (owner, 2026-10-17). Six rules, from the owner's review of L1–L3:**
> 1. **Test the point itself.** A measure-word group makes the learner pick the measure word (with 兩/二 as the number trap), not just the number.
> 2. **Build multi-part patterns, one slot per part:** V不V (verb + 不 + verb, with 嗎 and 沒 as traps), 有沒有, number + measure word. A lone 不/沒 blank is too easy. Tiles may repeat (熱 twice) as often as slots use them.
> 3. **No artefacts in sentences:** never type the book's picture caption or pinyin (`（rè）`) into a line; captions live in the picture crop or `glossary`.
> 4. **No repeats:** an own item must test a mistake nothing else in the group covers (a test fails an own item with the same tiles as a book item).
> 5. **Position particles use tap-where (`gaps: true`)**, with a repair per wrong gap (的 都 也 吧 嗎 在 有), never fill-in with a silly tile like 是.
> 6. **Check every lesson for the same flaw**, not only the screen that was reported.
> 7. **The title is the item's own question** (`prompt`, e.g. "When does your class end today?"), never the book's general instruction, and never a giveaway. Extra context goes in `sub`.
> 8. **At least 3 options**, each a real taught form that is right somewhere (不有 / 不是 for 沒有, 是 before an adjective, 的 for 得, 十多歲 vs 十歲多). Where the point is a pattern (time + action, 送 + person + gift, V得 + comment, 又…又…, VV看, 好/難 + V, 多 with measure words), the learner builds it, one slot per part.
> 9. **Every wrong tile explains itself wherever it lands** (a `placed` note, or a `slot` note per wrong slot). 
> 10. **A doubled tile must not give the answer away:** when the pattern doubles a word the context chooses (坐坐看), double a plausible wrong word too (騎騎, with a repair), and let the item's title decide which fits. 
> 11. **Frames are tap-where too:** when the learner must place two or more words in a sentence (快要…了, 又…又), use `gaps: true` with several tiles (a tile per right gap, a repair for every wrong tile in every wrong gap) instead of blanks that show where each word goes. Tests enforce 1, 7, 8 and 9.

> **Pipeline runs are non-interactive and pre-approved by the owner.** When a task comes from `.audit/grammar-run`, do it fully now: skip the plan-first / "wait for go" steps of `task-start` and `AGENTS.md`, and don't ask questions (write them under Unsure).

## Exercise prompt rule (owner, 2026-10-24)

Short English task instructions are allowed. The actual problem must come from the book's Chinese question, source sentence, dialogue or picture; do not supply a full English answer translation or narrate the pictured sequence/comparison before answering. Restore original Chinese context using existing `sub`/`lines` fields. For open-ended book questions, keep a minimal practice-situation cue if needed to make grading fair; do not invent a source fact or silently demand one answer. Matching always shows its instruction. Source sentences for rewrite/rearrange tasks stay in Chinese. Feedback may show the full English meaning. This rule overrides older exercise instructions below to preserve printed English verbatim; grammar reference pages still preserve the printed text.

## Content rules (strict; the owner's law)

**Scope — nothing the learner hasn't been taught**
1. **Words:** only Book 1 vocabulary up to this lesson *part* (B1L01-2-xx words are Part 2: not allowed in a Part 1 exercise), plus words in the book's own grammar examples so far. Names and words the book itself prints in that exercise go in `allowWords`. A word the book prints beside the exercise goes in `allowWords`; nothing else does. `tests/content/grammarPracticeVocabulary.test.ts` enforces it.
2. **Grammar:** only grammar points taught *before or at this point, in book order*. An exercise for L1 point 3 may not use L1 point 4 (呢). Patterns from a later part of the same lesson count as untaught. The scope list (`.audit/grammar-run/scope.md`) shows what's taught and what's not yet. Watch the patterns made of known words: progressive 在 (L9), completed-action 了 (L11), 是…的 (L11), 先…再 (L9), comparisons (L9+), 就 (L10+).
3. **Book text only on pages:** tables and examples exactly as printed in the transcript. No invented example sentences anywhere on a page (clarifiers and labs may only re-show book sentences).

**Simplicity — make learners learn, no clutter**
4. **Produce the grammar, not a revealed blank (owner, 2026-10-18):** when sentence structure is the lesson, use `order-build` for book items as well as own items. Keep the book sentence and context, but require the learner to assemble the target structure on a free line. Keep grammar-bearing words separate (可以 + action, verb + 得 + comment, number + 多 + measure word). Use `gaps: true` for longer frames and particle position so underlines never supply the answer positions. Keep targeted choices when the choice itself is the lesson (classifier, demonstrative, picture-dependent meaning). Never supply the target grammar and test an unrelated vocabulary blank instead. Stay within six short tiles; do not split long clauses just to create a sentence puzzle.
5. **Short tiles:** at most 4 characters per tile and at most 6 tiles per dock (the validator enforces both). Prefer 3–5 tiles.
6. **At least one tempting wrong tile** that targets the pattern's typical mistake, with a one-sentence repair. It must really be wrong in context; if a "wrong" tile could also be right, accept it or drop it.
7. **The prompt defines the required answer:** do not secretly require a background or trailing clause the prompt already supplies. Preserve the full book sentence as the canonical model and list natural concise answers in `accepted` when they retain the taught grammar and requested meaning. Each accepted answer needs its own sentence, reading and translation (`result`/`alternatives` for slots, `accepted[].sentence` for builds). Keep exact recording keys for listening. An optional `successNote` explains the target briefly after a correct build. Repairs describe the actual error; never tell the learner to put 好 before 看 when that relation is already correct.
8. **Fit the format to the book item; never force a template.** For each exercise, ask what the learner must notice (where a connector goes, a guess vs a question, now vs soon) and pick the kind that shows exactly that with the least clutter: `slot-build` (place words into the book sentence, optionally with a scene; with `gaps: true` the learner taps WHERE one word goes: every slot is a gap between words, only one lists the word, and each wrong gap has a `slot` repair saying why — use it when the book tests word position, e.g. 嗎/呢/也/都/了), `chat-build` (the book says "rearrange"), or `slot-reels` (the book says "write your own"). Vary across a lesson only when the book item differs. **Book items may use `order-build` (build the original sentence on a free line) when structure is the lesson. Own items (after the book's) may also use `spot-fix` (tap the wrong word of a friend's sentence, then fix it), `match-pairs` (reason ↔ result), `sort-groups` (one word, two jobs), `story-order` (sentences sequenced by connectors or time words) `listen-build` (hear one clause of a book dialogue, then build it; the clip is cut from `content/dialogueAlignment.json` as the template's "Cutting a clip" says, and one order is right) and `listen-pick` (hear a clip, tap which of 3–4 written sentences was said; they differ only in the point's sound, each wrong one with a `note`).** Pick one only for the need it serves, and follow `docs/GRAMMAR_LESSON_TEMPLATE.md` → Where each kind goes (it also lists the plan for L9–L16): a word-order pattern, a real learner mistake, logic that links two lines, a word with two jobs, a sequence. Rules that carry over: taught words only, one right answer decided by context (list every grammatical order in `accepted`), a `placed` repair for every wrong tile, no repeat of a mistake the group already tests, and no giveaway (a `hint` never names the wrong word; a match always has a trap reply). If no existing kind fits well, do not force one: leave that exercise out and write a `NEEDS-ENGINE:` line in `docs/grammar/reviews/LXX-needs.md` describing the interaction you would want (what the learner sees, taps and gets back). The design lead builds it.

**Faithfulness**
9. Follow the book's exercise: its instruction (`title`), its items in order, its pictures (`scene`: route / change / countdown). Preserve the original sentence, source, context and learning objective; the answering interaction may change to remove giveaways. Do not replace a book item with invented content. An open blank gets the most natural completion built from taught words, plus the accepted equivalents.
10. Keep the book's printed English on reference pages; exercise prompts follow the owner rule above. If printed English misleads, flag it as BOOK-ERRATA in the review rather than rewriting it.
11. Pinyin uses the book's tones, 一/不 sandhi, and neutral reduplication (看看 kàn kan).
14. **Learner-facing text only:** `title` is the book's exercise instruction in English (e.g. "Complete the sentences."), never "Exercise". `prompt`/`sub` speak to the learner ("Use 叫 or 姓.") or are omitted; never authoring notes like "The verb is supplied."
12. Repair notes are one plain-English sentence that names the fix. Explanations are short and paraphrase the book's note. Pattern labels follow `docs/GRAMMAR_LESSON_TEMPLATE.md`.
13a. **Never merge tables:** when the book prints several small tables (e.g. 月/號, 星期, 點/分), keep each as its own subsection with its own columns. Every printed cell (units like 號 and 分 included) appears, and no row has an empty slot that the book doesn't have.
13. Grammar pages mirror the book's layout: tables become `patternRows`/`subsections` (the book's (1)/(2)), and numbered sentences become `examples`. L8 (`content/grammar/lessonEight.json`) is the reference for page shape.

## Exercise design: be a good teacher (owner's standard, 2026-10-16)

Prototype to match: https://claude.ai/artifact/44QEgBfQtapzARTBjZe4hE

0. **Every book exercise item first, none dropped.** Each item in the book's 練習 boxes becomes one app item, in book order, with `source` naming it (e.g. "p.81 #3"); a coverage check fails on any missing one. Make each one smart: keep the book's sentence, and add the context (its dialogue line, its picture via the book-picture scene) that makes exactly one answer right. Only then add 1–3 of your own items per point, for a typical mistake the book items don't already test. Never drop a book item because it has a picture; describe the crop it needs in the review instead.
1. **Start from the mistake.** For each grammar point, name the 2–3 mistakes learners really make (the book's note plus common errors: 四星期 for 星期四, the day before the month, 嗎 after a question word, 很 before a noun). Every item tests exactly one of them.
2. **Exactly one right answer, decided by context.** The dialogue, sentence or picture makes one option right. If a second option could also be right, change the context or the option.
3. **Smart options:** at least three real, taught choices when choosing a fill; sentence builds and tap-where frames use the tiles the pattern needs, within the six-tile dock. Use real, taught forms that are each right somewhere (嗎/呢, 叫/姓/是, 很/不是), so the learner has to think. Never a nonsense distractor.
4. **Three forms, picked per mistake:**
   - **Choose:** pick the one word for the gap.
   - **Order:** arrange 2–3 short tiles; only one order is right (星期四, 五月十六號, 九點二十分). Add one trap tile.
   - **Read:** a calendar, clock, route or before/now picture decides the answer.
   - **Spot, Match, Sort, Story:** noticing a real mistake, linking logic, sorting a word's two jobs, sequencing by connectors (own items only; see rule 8).
   - **Listen:** hearing a word that sounds like another or is its opposite, then building the clause (own items only; see rule 8).
4b. **No giveaways:** the answer must not be copyable from the context (B saying 她叫友美 gives away 叫, so B just says 友美。) or findable by elimination. All options look alike: same kind of word, similar length, all plausible here. If two options are obviously wrong, replace them.
5. **Every wrong option teaches:** its own one-sentence "why", naming what the context shows ("16 is under 四, not 三").
6. **3–5 items per grammar point**, mixing the forms. Later points in a lesson mix in earlier contrasts (嗎 vs 呢 once 呢 is taught).
7. **Build from the book:** its dialogues, names and words. Scope rules 1–2 apply strictly.
